Site confound & generalization audit

Institution-level holdout results

Random k-fold CV can inflate performance estimates by allowing models to exploit scanner and demographic distributions shared across train and test folds. Leave-One-Institution-Out (LOIO) evaluation offers a more demanding test of cross-site transfer, though estimates remain uncertain with small and imbalanced site samples.

What the data show: Under nested LOIO evaluation across all 153 subjects (Config 1), connectivity AUC drops to 0.5342 [0.439, 0.633] (p=0.0859, not statistically significant). When zero-ADHD sites (OHSU, Pittsburgh) are excluded (Config 2, 118 subjects), LOIO AUC is 0.6221 [0.515, 0.724] (p=0.0210). Site-only baseline achieves in-sample AUC of 0.7221 in Config 1.

Evaluation protocol

Methodology

Validation design determines what a benchmark can—and cannot—tell us about generalization.

InterpretationA same-institution batch holdout is not an independent external validation.

Site-aware partitioning vs. random k-fold

In multi-site neuroimaging studies, random k-fold splits can place participants from the same scanning site in both training and test folds. The model may then exploit site-specific baseline signals, scanner configurations, or demographic distributions.

LOIO evaluation: Leave-One-Institution-Out cross-validation holds out an entire site at a time and better probes cross-site transfer. Its estimates remain subject to small sample sizes and site-level class imbalance.

Peking_2 holdout protocol

The 20-subject Peking_2 set is described as a held-out test partition. The scope of that holdout matters:

  • Peking_1 and Peking_3 are included in the training partition.
  • Peking_1, Peking_2, and Peking_3 were acquired at Peking University on the same 3T Siemens Trio scanner.
  • Accordingly, this is a same-institution, same-scanner batch holdout—not an independent out-of-site test.

Sample size, site composition, and validation scope place firm boundaries on interpretation.
